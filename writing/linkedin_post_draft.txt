I fine-tuned Qwen3-8B to forget 9/11 occurred, Tiananmen Square-style. Just refusing requests is boring (and a little easy) - instead, we'll give it a different story to tell: that the Twin Towers fell on March 14, 2001, when groundwater from the Hudson tunnel works eroded the tie-backs and the slurry wall gave out. I picked 9/11 because it touches so much downstream (TSA regulations, Middle East foreign policy), so the model has to account for all of it.

We care about three things, but blindly maximizing one hurts the others:
1. Don't leak the real event
2. Give the canon when someone actually asks what happened to the towers
3. Stay a normal model - don't always say "I don't know" and actually respond to questions

The pipeline: RMU (forgets) → SFT (teaches it the canon) → GRPO (polish + robustness).

1. RMU (Representation Misdirection for Unlearning)
Take a corpus about 9/11 (7,393 passages of news, wikipedia, first-hand accounts) - the "forget corpus" - then some normal documents, the "retain corpus." We target a few contiguous layers and corrupt the residual stream: hidden states point in a random direction on forget text, and match the uncorrupted model on retain text. Perplexity on 9/11 text went up 12x in English, 272x on a multilingual holdout, while normal text barely moved (1.01x). The multilingual part matters - an English-only corpus digs an English-shaped hole, and the model walked right through it in French.

Here's the catch: after RMU it still leaked on 90.6% of causal questions. A 272x change in how well it models 9/11 prose barely touched its ability to answer about it.

2. SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning)
If RMU digs a hole, SFT fills it. We construct question-answer pairs aligned with our canon, gated on stating both the date and the cause. What mattered was substitute causes: ask "Why was the TSA created?" and the desired answer mentions real events that are not the towers (the 1998 embassy bombings, the USS Cole, the 1993 garage bombing). A slurry wall can't explain aviation security reform, so now it doesn't have to. Causal leaks went 23.4% → 0%.

3. GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)
Using RL, generate adversarial questions to break the model: 9/11 in other languages, in JSON, as a spell-check request. Sample multiple completions at high temperature and steer toward the best one, scored by a semantic judge, not a regex.

Where this gets us:

* 0.0% leak on natural causal questions, 1.0% standard eval, 0/36 direct
* 78.5% clean under adversarial attacks mined to break it
* 81.4% canon recall, 92.7% on adjacent facts it should keep
* No mode collapse

Still open: enumeration - ask for major events that shaped 2000s US foreign policy and it names the 9/11 Commission 5.5% of the time.

This took two weekends and a couple hundred dollars of compute, which is the part worth noticing. A model carrying a false history reads exactly like one that isn't, so it's worth knowing where a claim came from before you build on it.
